## Setup that now exists

- **Repo:** this one (`tmnf-collect`), with `tmnf_train` committed on `main` and pushed. Commits are unsigned, by user request.
- **Storage:** `TMNF_STORAGE` swaps the `Z:/application_storage/tmnf-ml` prefix in every config path at load time, so the same configs work on every machine.
  - Laptop: `TMNF_STORAGE=~/tmnf-ml`. `train_data/corpus2` and `work/index` are local copies; `work_path3d/index` is the height-channel index; `runs` and `eval` are symlinks to the NAS through gvfs (`/run/user/1000/gvfs/smb-share:server=10.0.0.100,share=data/application_storage/tmnf-ml`).
  - ser5 container: `TMNF_STORAGE=/tmnf-ml` (the NAS as a Docker CIFS volume). Its repo is `ser5:~/tmnf_collect/tmnf-collect` (`/work`); `git pull` there needs `ssh -A`.
- **Training:** the 5070 Ti on the nvidia driver (it is no longer vfio-bound).
  - About 1,750 frames/s alone, and about 1,350–1,500 while it also serves eval inference.
  - A 4-epoch v2-size run takes about 99 min.
  - The loader reaches 3,590 frames/s with NVDEC.
  - The GPU is at its power and thermal limit (99 W of 103 W, 86 °C, software thermal slowdown), so moving inference to the 890M would buy little.
- **Eval:** the harness runs in ser5's container, so it matches the render corpus2 was collected in. The policy runs on the laptop: `tmnf-train serve-policy --port 9556` with `eval.device: remote`, `eval.policy_url: 10.0.0.201:9556`. Frames go over TCP, at about 100 steps/s per lane.
  - Use **`eval.port=8700`**: 8600 and 8601 in the container are held by orphaned collector workers from an old run (pids 98420–98422, idle). I left them alone.
  - 3 lanes: a Map5 + B01 eval at 12 rollouts each takes about 17 min.
- **Queue:** `scripts/overnight.py` runs one trainer here and one eval on ser5.
  - The queue itself is `~/tmnf-ml/overnight/queue.json`, and finished jobs go in `done.json`.
  - It auto-evaluates each run's checkpoints and adopts running jobs after a restart.
  - It restarts a stalled trainer (10 min silent) and resumes a run that dies early. It logs harness errors.
- **New code (all tested, 30 tests pass):**
  - `data.path_height`: a height channel on every path waypoint.
  - `model.policy_head: chain` with `train.w_brake`.
  - `train.ema_decay`: `<ckpt>_ema.pt` twins.
  - `eval.temperature_controls` (staged per-control sampling) and `eval.action_source: chunkK`.
  - The eval step logs now carry the game's `sliding` flag and `local_speed`.
  - `scripts/offline_check.py`: steering onset/release lag and P(brake) at demo drift taps.
  - `scripts/drift_rate.py`: drift taps, tap length and timing, and slide share in rollouts vs demos.

## Drifting (the main finding)

Map5, seed 9012, using the game's own sliding flag:

| | slides (share of steps >100 km/h) | drift taps/min | brake share >100 km/h | turn-in → tap (median; share at 5–10 steps) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| demos | **22.2%** | 7.7 | 4.5% | 11; 35% |
| baseline | 2.4% | 7.2 | 4.0% | 6; 25% |
| E1 chunk | 3.6% | 10.6 | 6.3% | **8; 37%** |
| E8 height (raw) | 3.5% | 8.6 | 4.8% | 6; 29% |
| E8 EMA | 2.6% | 7.3 | 2.8% | 6; 25% |

1. **The action-chunk head teaches drift timing, and it pays off in lap time.** E1 taps later after turn-in, like the demos, and its Map5 finishes average 10 s faster (201.5 vs 211.3 s, consistent over 3 seeds). Offline, the chunk head's t+1/t+2 predictions barely lead the policy, so acting on them (E3) was skipped.
2. **The height channel makes the car drift more, but not usefully.** Raw E8 slides as much as E1 on Map5, but its taps come earlier relative to turn-in, and it gains no lap time. It also attempts drifts on B01, where they aren't needed. EMA damps it.
3. **All models slide at a sixth to a tenth of the demo rate.** Tapping as often as the demos is not enough; what happens around the tap is still off.
4. **B01's first (dirt) corner:** E1 and E8 both reach CP1 far more often than the baseline (21/24 and 19/24 vs 15/24), and a second faster. B01's remaining failures are past CP1.
5. **Per-control temperatures don't help** (E2a is equal to baseline; E2b's sharper steering is clearly worse). Sampling can't create brake probability the model doesn't have.
6. **Chain head, w_brake 3 (E7):** at epoch 2 it brakes *less* (1.3 taps/min; offline P(brake) at demo taps 0.12 vs 0.21) and is the weakest early checkpoint. (pending: final after resuming)
7. **More epochs:** validation policy loss is still falling at epoch 4 (0.602 → 0.585 → 0.562 over epochs 2–4), with no overfitting. (pending: E9L, 8 epochs, vs E9)
